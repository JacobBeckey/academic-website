Outreach

A scientist's work belongs to the community that funds it.

I believe the people who do science have an obligation to share its tools — not just its results — with the educators, students, and public who make the work possible. Most of my outreach is built around supporting the K–12 teachers on whose shoulders this entire enterprise stands, and around mentoring the next generation of undergraduate researchers.

Three commitments

My outreach work is organized around three audiences whose access to authentic STEM practice I care most about.

I.

K–12 teachers

Building the professional development infrastructure that helps practicing teachers bring authentic scientific and mathematical practice into their classrooms.

II.

Undergraduate researchers

Mentoring undergraduates through serious, publishable research projects — and supporting the institutional structures, like the Illinois Mathematics Lab, that make this possible.

III.

Broader community

Tutoring, mentoring, and outreach to elementary students, graduate students, and the broader public on what science actually is and how it works.

Centerpiece

K–12 teacher professional development

Most of my outreach effort goes here. Teachers are the leverage point for science education at every level — and they deserve the same kind of intellectual community, professional support, and research-informed practice that university faculty take for granted. I co-founded two organizations to help build that infrastructure.

Co-founder & Vice President 2023–present
Idealized Science Institute
A national non-profit empowering physics educators with research-backed practice.

I co-founded the Idealized Science Institute (ISI) to give physics teachers a place to engage with the actual research on how science is learned, taught, and understood. ISI brings the findings of decades of educational research to the people who need them most — the educators in K–12 classrooms.

Free educational videos on the nature of science, scientific practice, and pedagogy.
A podcast that translates education research for practicing teachers, in plain language.
In-person professional development workshops, including a regional science conference held annually.
A curated K–12 STEM book library — the basis for our ongoing literacy-in-STEM initiative.

Co-founder & Lead Facilitator 2025–present
Central Illinois Math Teacher Circles
A community of practice for pre- and in-service math teachers in Central Illinois.

Math teacher circles bring practicing K–12 mathematics teachers together with research mathematicians to do mathematics — really do it — collaboratively. The model is built on decades of evidence that teachers teach better when they themselves remain active mathematical learners.

I co-founded the Central Illinois chapter to bring this model to teachers across the region, with monthly meetings hosted at UIUC.

Monthly professional development sessions for pre- and in-service teachers.
Collaborative problem-solving with research mathematicians and peer educators.
A regional community of math teachers across Central Illinois.

Mentorship

Undergraduate research mentorship

Many of my research projects involve undergraduate co-authors. At UIUC, much of this happens through the Illinois Mathematics Lab, which pairs mathematics undergraduates with faculty and postdoctoral mentors for serious semester- and year-long research projects.

I take undergraduate research seriously. The students I mentor do real work — proving theorems, running simulations, deriving bounds, writing and revising drafts — and several have become co-authors on papers in Phys. Rev. A and elsewhere. Those papers are marked with a ★ on the research page.

I work with students who are at very different stages of their training. Some are first-time researchers learning what mathematical research looks like from the inside; others are advanced undergraduates ready to drive a project independently. Both are welcome.

If you are a URI undergraduate curious about quantum information theory, mathematics, or theoretical physics, please get in touch. You do not need to have taken a quantum mechanics course to start.
